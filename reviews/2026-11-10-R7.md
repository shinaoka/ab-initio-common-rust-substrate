## modules/gpu-mixed-precision.md - fixed

- Checked against the sources: device ratio threshold 8 and the AMD table (data-centre gfx9 parts 2, consumer parts 16);
  double-float default keyed to the ratio for grid integrals and orbital evaluation; about 10 FP32 operations per term;
  RTX 5080 timings (1.42 -> 0.61 s, 0.77 -> 0.41 s, 0.37 -> 0.098 s); GEMMul8 defaults (15 moduli, range 2-20, accurate
  scaling, 30 % / 1.5 GiB / 256 MiB); A100 rule n >= 6144 and 7-21 % gain; projector-rounding offset 1.627e-7 Ha;
  plain-FP32 grid mode (1e-5, 27-40 vs 23 steps); refinement: warm threshold 2e-5, delta formula and first-step
  freezing, warm floor, occupation test 1e-12, K = 2, complement rotation, chain 30 delta, cluster limit
  min(2048, sqrt(n k_c / 2)), host solve below 256, about 1e9-flop switch, failure scopes (first SCF step transient),
  FP64 re-solve of the final step, stop guard 100 x scf.criterion, exclusions, order 1000 limit on AMD only; AMD phase
  similarity (golden ratio, 1e-25).
- Changes:
  - Scope: commit 08d0521 stated.
  - Notation: the symbol X_c was used both for the complement columns (4.3) and the cluster columns (4.4). Cluster
    columns are now \\( X\_{\mathcal C} \\); \\( f\_i \\) added.
  - Section 1, keyword (factual): `scf.fp32.enable off` does not disable the emulated GEMM or cuSOLVER's FP64 emulation.
    - Before: "`scf.fp32.enable off` disables every optional reduced-precision path"
    - After: "`scf.fp32.enable off` disables the optional FP32 and double-float paths (grid integrals, orbital evaluation, FP32 eigensolve with refinement); it does not affect the emulated GEMM or the vendor FP64 emulation, which have their own settings."
  - Section 2: [Public] references added for double-float arithmetic (Dekker 1971) and error-free transformations
    (Ogita, Rump, Oishi 2005).
  - Section 3, workspace (factual): the 256 MiB cap applies to every process, not only to processes sharing a device;
    the 30 % budget is divided among the sharing processes and is 50 % in the noncollinear band solver.
  - Section 3, A100: "11 of 12 other product types" -> "in a benchmark of 12 general products (real and complex,
    workspace capped), native FP64 was faster in 11" (it is 11 of 12 measured shapes).
  - Section 3, accuracy (factual): the quoted whole-program figures (1.8e-10 Ha, 9.6e-9 Ha/bohr) come from a regression
    comparing two GEMMul8 versions, partly in CPU mode, and do not measure the effect of emulation. Replaced by the strict
    cold-start comparison of native-FP64 and default-policy runs (2.6e-11 Ha, 5e-12 Ha/bohr, same SCF step count).
  - Section 4 restructured (see Common checks). Before/after of the core:
    - Before (4.3 step 1-2): "Y = A X1, S = X^dagger Y, G = X^dagger X1, all with the emulated GEMM ... lambda_j = S_jj/G_jj for the refined columns; R = I - G."
    - After: "S = X^dagger A X1 and R = I - X^dagger X1 (both n x k_c). Estimates lambda_j = S_jj/(1 - R_jj) for j <= k_c; the unrefined states keep their current estimates." (preceded by the [Public] Ogita-Aishima step, 4.0)
    - Before (4.4): "Walk the sorted lambda and chain consecutive values closer than 30 delta into clusters. For each cluster of size >= 2 that contains different occupations, ..."
    - After: "The estimates lambda_1..lambda_kc (in index order) are partitioned into maximal runs in which neighbouring estimates differ by at most 30 delta. A run of two or more states whose occupations, re-evaluated at the refined estimates, span a range of at least 10^-12 is a cluster."
    - Added facts: both updates of a step use the basis from the start of the step; the "fallback to native GEMM ends
      refinement" rule applies only when refinement is on by default.
  - Section 4.2, band passes (near copy of the environment-variable reference).
    - Before: "In band calculations the estimates of all k-points are gathered before the chemical potential is set; when a process holds several k-points, a first pass refines with the previous step's chemical potential and a second pass refines again with the new one."
    - After: "In band calculations the chemical potential is determined from the estimates of all k-points together. If one process holds more than one k-point, each of its problems is refined twice: once using the chemical potential of the preceding SCF step, and again, starting from that result, once the current chemical potential is known."
  - Section 4.3, complement rationale (near copy of a source comment).
    - Before: "the refined columns converge to a fixed point tilted out of the occupied subspace by about 10^-8"
    - After: "the FP32-level components of X_c along the occupied subspace leave the refined columns converging to vectors that deviate from that subspace by about 10^-8"
  - Section 4.5: serialized spin mode added to the exclusions.
  - Section 4.6: "observed with ROCm 7.2" added; [Open] now asks about later libraries.
  - Section 4.7 (factual): the two-stage FP32 timing 323 vs 638 ms is from the RTX 5080 (n = 5616), not the A100; the
    reason refinement was not adopted in the distributed path is the cost of the distributed products (four or more per
    step, each costing about what the FP32 solve saves), not data redistribution. A100 warm-start timing kept, with the
    note that refinement is not on by default there.
- Remaining concerns:
  - The citation of part II of Ogita-Aishima (JJIAM 36, 435-459, 2019) was written from memory; the maintainer should
    confirm volume and pages.
  - Section 4.1 step 1 (symmetrizing from the lower triangle) and the 200 bisection halvings are implementation details
    with negligible effect on results; kept because they are stated as conventions, not control flow.
